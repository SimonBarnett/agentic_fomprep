# Warehouse Assembly Wizard

 

An assembly document is used to report the assembly of child parts (components) into a parent (assembled) part in the warehouse. If you are working with serial numbers, you can report the assembly of a serialized part from serialized components. 
 

Reports of assemblies are considered inventory transactions: reported assembly of a part automatically increases its inventory balance, on the one hand, and decreases the inventory balance of its components, on the other. 
 

The assembly document is also used as an aid to the warehouse manager, who can schedule workers for required assemblies, and as an aid to workers, indicating the amount of work hours allocated to each assembly, and the quantity of components needed for each assembled parent. It also serves as a report of the actual amount of time spent by each worker on an assembly. 
 

The components required for a given assembled parent, as well as their required quantities, are determined by a bill of materials (BOM). You can use the parent's standard BOM, a custom BOM recorded in a sales order, or a custom BOM recorded in the assembly document itself. Example. 
 

If ordered parts need to be assembled, you can automatically create an assembly document on the basis of a sales order. This will not only save time in itemizing the assembly document, but also allow you to keep track of the assembly of such ordered items.
