# Cost Allocation Wizard

 

Using Priority 's cost allocation facility, you can analyze the profitability and costs of various portions of your enterprise, by means of profit and cost centers . 
 

A profit center is an organizational unit to which revenues and direct and indirect costs can be assigned. For example, you can define each of your company's departments as a profit center, and compare their profitability. Alternatively, you can define each product line, or each major customer, as a profit center. 
 

A cost center represents a source of expense (e.g., rent, insurance, legal fees) that must be distributed among the profit centers in order to provide an accurate picture of their profitability. These are referred to as indirect costs , relative to the profit centers to which they are allocated. 
 

Each profit center must be assigned to one of five profit groups . Each group consists of a different type of profit center, in order to enable you to analyze your company's profitability from several different angles. Example. Similarly, cost centers are organized into cost groups , according to the profit groups in which their expenses are allocated. 
 

Within each profit/cost group, you can create a hierarchical tree of profit or cost centers, which enables you to analyze costs, revenues and profit at up to three levels. Example.
