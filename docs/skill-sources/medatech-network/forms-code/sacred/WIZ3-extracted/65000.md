# Getting Started Main Wizard

 

Welcome to Priority . 
 

This wizard will assist you as you initialize the system. It does not detail every single option provided by the system, but focuses on those definitions and data that are needed to start working on a regular basis. If you require additional help, you can use the other main wizards ( Purchasing , Sales , Inventory, Customer Service, Human Resources, Basic Accounting and Advanced Accounting ), as well as the topical wizards and online help that are available throughout the system. 
 

For your convenience, this wizard is divided into sections, each dealing a different area requiring initialization: opening a company, accounting, inventory, sales, purchasing and setting constants. 
 
## Using This Wizard

 

The following sections outline various aspects of system initialization. Click the hypertext links to open specialized wizards designed to assist you in the performance of specific tasks. 
 
## Opening a Company

 

When the Priority system is installed, it automatically sets up a dummy company containing minimal data, for demonstration purposes. Your first action before you can record actual data in the system is to open a real company that will store this data. After opening the company, you need to define the system's users , assign them privileges by means of the Privilege Explorer and open personnel files . 
 
## Importing Data

 

Use Priority 's data migration module to import existing data from your old system. 
 

Once customers are set up, synchronize Priority with MS-Outlook to import existing customer contacts and leads. 
 
## Accounting

 

Before you begin working with Priority , verify that all currencies you require are defined in the system, and record their current exchange rates. Then define the properties of financial statements and set up general ledger accounts and default accounts . 
 

Next, open A/R accounts in a separate subledger. You can open accounts for regular customers and for walk-in customers (a group of one-time or infrequent customers, which share a common ledger account). Also record potential customers (without a ledger account); they can later be converted into regular customers. 
 

Similarly, open A/P accounts in a separate subledger. Here, too, you can open accounts for regular vendors and one-time vendors . Customers that are also vendors can be managed in a special type of account. 
 

 Note: For all ledgers (GL, A/R and A/P) you can define secured accounts and hide them from unauthorized users. 
 

Once you have set up the ledgers, you need to set up your company's cashiers , and define bank accounts both for your company and for your customers and vendors (for the purpose of bank transfers). You also need to set up the credit card companies with which your company works. 
 

To record financial transactions in the ledger, you need to define fiscal years and periods . Then record opening balances for each account. 
 

In the U.S. package, your next tasks are to set up for sales tax and assign tax codes to your customers. Then review the payment terms predefined by the system and adapt them to your needs. 
 

In the dual-currency package, your next tasks are to set up VAT , withholding tax and tax prepayment definitions , as well as payment terms . 
 

Financial operations in Priority are recorded in financial documents. You must define each document type's numeration template and other properties (for example, whether closing the document automatically posts a journal entry for that transaction). Each type of financial document is linked to a predefined journal entry code, which appears automatically when recording a document. However, you can define alternative journal entry codes , entry codes for manual journal entries and unique entry codes for each company branch or customer/vendor. The use of entry codes makes it easier to record transactions in the ledger, and allows you to retrieve types of transaction documents by their codes. 
 
## Inventory

 

In order to maintain inventory levels, it is important to open records for your warehouses . 
 

The correct definition of parts is crucial to the efficient operation of the system, because different Priority modules relate to different part parameters. For any parent part (e.g., an assembled part), you also need to define the bill of materials . Finally, you can also link each part to a part family , for purchasing and sales purposes (e.g., shared prices) and to an accounting family , for various financial purposes (e.g., shared income accounts). 
 

To initialize the system's inventory of parts, you must perform an initial inventory count . This count creates inventory of the parts stored in your warehouses before work with the system has begun. Then "lock" the inventory balances recorded in the count by calculating past balances for the date on which the count took place. By recording past balances, you prevent any retroactive changes in inventory levels, which is crucial to future costing and inventory calculations. 
 

Inventory control in Priority is managed through inventory documents. As with financial documents, you need to define their numeration templates and other properties (for e.g., default warehouse). 
 
## Sales

 

After opening records for your company's customers, you can define a variety of price lists, discounts and bonuses for parts and part families, and link them to specific customers. You can also define credit limits for your customers, and track their credit profile. 
 
## Purchasing

 

 Vendor parts and prices: After defining the vendors with which your company works, you can define each vendor's part list. This list includes the vendor's part numbers and special units of measurement, if any. You can then create vendor price lists for these parts, which can contain different prices for different quantities or manufacturers. If you purchase all parts from the same vendor, and are not interested in tracking price histories, you can simply record the vendor price as the part's default price, and avoid working with vendor price lists altogether. 
 
## Business Process Management (BPM)

 

To maintain control over business processes, it is recommended that you use BPM flow charts to define permitted sequences of status allocation, as well as create status rules, for each type of document. The system provides a list of predefined statuses for each relevant type of document and assigns them default properties. In addition to creating status paths and rules, you can add new statuses and revise existing properties. For detailed information, see the User Interface Guide . 
 
## Calendar Appointments

 

The Priority Calendar interfaces with its task facility. Any tasks that cover a period of time (where end time is later than start time) are automatically opened as appointments in the Calendar. Conversely, any appointments opened directly in the Calendar are automatically included in the Tasks form. For more information on Calendar options, see also the User Interface Guide .
