# Purchasing Main Wizard

 
## Introduction

 

 Priority provides you with the tools you need to plan and procure your material requirements efficiently and accurately. For example: 
 

1)  Open records for those vendors with which you do business. Assign them quality levels and rank them in order of preference as suppliers of particular parts. 
 

2)  Create lists of the parts supplied by each vendor, and their catalogue prices, if available. 
 

3)  Run the Material Requirements Planning (MRP) program to determine which goods you need to purchase in order to fill your regular orders as well as anticipated incoming orders. 
 

4)  Run the Purchase Planning program in order to generate purchase requisitions for those items identified by MRP. 
 

5)  Use the purchase requisitions you have generated to open requests for price quotes for the items in question, and send them to the relevant vendors. 
 

6)  Record the price quotes that you receive from the vendors, and run a comparison report to determine the best offers. 
 

7)  Open purchase orders (including blanket purchase orders) for the required items and send them to the appropriate vendors. 
 

8)  As you receive goods from your vendors, record their entry into inventory and perform any required laboratory testing on item samples. If you are invoiced for the shipment, record both the financial and inventory transaction together in a Vendor Invoice. 
 

9)  If any of the goods were rejected after testing, or need to be returned for any other reason, ship them back to the vendor together with a debit memo, requesting compensation for the returned parts. 
 
## Note:

 

Use various BPM tools to track the progress of purchasing documents, streamline the purchasing process and increase the flow of information between employees handling these documents. 
 
## Using this wizard

 

The following sections outline various aspects of purchasing. 
 

Click the hypertext links to open specialized wizards designed to assist you in the performance of specific purchasing tasks. Each wizard features an introductory page providing a brief overview of the subject, followed by detailed instructions. Most of these wizards are also accessible from the main Priority Purchasing menu and its sub-menus. 
 
## Vendors

 

 Priority allows you to open records for your regular suppliers, common ledger account for one-time vendors, as well as a special kind of ledger account for vendors who are also customers. You can also create a custom template for the automatic numeration of new vendor records. Finally, you can define secured vendors and hide them from unauthorized users. 
 
## Vendor Parts and Prices

 

Once you have recorded a vendor, you can create a list of parts supplied by that vendor. This list will contain the vendor's part numbers, as well as any special units of measure used by the vendor. You can also assign a preferred vendor per part and rank the various vendors that sell the same part. Moreover, you can utilize a quality control mechanism to limit vendors to those that have a sufficiently high quality level. 
 

 Note: Vendor part lists and rankings are used by the system to prepare requests for price quotes automatically from purchase requisitions (see Vendor Price Quotes below). 
 

Once you have defined a list of parts for a vendor, you can create price lists for those parts containing the vendor's prices and discounts, including separate prices for different minimum quantities or different manufacturers. If you purchase all of your parts from a single vendor and are not interested in maintaining a history of prices, you can simply assign the vendor's price to each part as its default purchase price, as an alternative to maintaining price lists. 
 
## Vendor Price Quotes

 

 Requests for Price Quotes (RFQs) can be generated automatically from purchase requisitions, either for preferred vendors only, for all vendors whose part list contains the requisitioned item, or for all vendors up to a specific ranking (see Vendors above). RFQs can also be prepared manually, in which case they can be itemized automatically from specific sales orders. In all cases, the vendor's quality level (general or special) must be sufficient to supply the part. 
 

After you have received responses from the vendors in question, you can record each vendor price quote manually, or itemize it automatically on the basis of an RFQ or a purchase demand document (of purchase requisitions). You can then compare the various quotes and select the best one to serve as the basis of a purchase order. 
 
## Material Requirements Planning (MRP)

 

Determining material requirements is the first step in the purchasing process. Whether your company manufactures a product or purchases goods for resale , accurate forecasts of future needs are essential to the efficient running of your business. 
 

 Priority utilizes the relevant system data (e.g., inventory levels, bills of materials, open sales orders, user-defined planning parameters) to automatically determine how much of which raw materials or products are needed and when, both for manufacturers and distributors. MRP results can then be used to generate purchase requisitions (see Purchase Planning below). 
 

 Note: The same process can also be used to generate work orders and issues plans for factory production of the required parts. It should be noted, however, that this process does not take into account production capabilities and other constraints. Manufacturers who choose to determine material requirements through finite capacity production planning can also use their results to generate purchase requisitions. 
 
## Purchase Planning

 

 Purchase planning is the process by which material requirements are translated into demands for parts. Purchase Requisitions (PRs) must be opened both for parts that are required in fixed quantities at regular intervals, and for parts whose requirements are planned dynamically on the basis of sales orders (by either MRP or production planning). PRs can be generated automatically on the basis of MRP results and system data, or recorded manually. Once a PR has been fully authorized, it can be used to generate requests for price quotes. You can then link quoted items to their corresponding PRs, thereby updating the latter with the price and other data from the quote. Finally, you can generate purchase orders automatically from the purchase demand document. 
 
## Purchase Orders

 

 Priority allows you to open a purchase order in a variety of ways: on the basis of a vendor price list, a vendor price quote, a purchase requisition, a blanket purchase order or a sales order. If you are recording a purchase order manually and there is no vendor price list, the system uses the part's defined purchase price, if any; otherwise it uses the last price recorded for the part in a purchase order. The price source is listed individually for each item in the purchase order. In the dual-currency package, order prices can be linked to a foreign currency , price index or currency basket. 
 

Purchase orders must be fully authorized before they can be sent to vendors. 
 
## Receipt of Goods

 

When the goods arrive from the vendor, you can record their receipt in a Goods Receiving Voucher (GRV), which records the entry of goods into inventory, or in a Vendor Invoice, which bills you for the goods in addition to recording the inventory transaction. 
 

If an incoming part is marked for laboratory testing, it is not released into inventory until it is tested and the test results are recorded in a Laboratory Release document. 
 

Both GRVs and Vendor Invoices can be used to record the return of goods to a vendor; in this case, quantities are recorded using negative values (although a special Returns of Goods form is also available for this purpose). 
 
## Invoicing

 

 Priority provides several methods of recording purchase invoices and memos . You can use a vendor invoice to both debit the sale and record the receipt of goods into inventory, or a multi-GRV invoice to collectively debit several received shipments. 
 

Open a debit memo against a vendor to request compensation for returned goods or overcharging, or a credit memo to record credit received from a vendor. 
 
## Commission Sales

 

You can document all stages of a commission sale (e.g., RFQs, commission orders, payment of commission), as well as set up commission sales price lists for each vendor. 
 
## Purchasing Reports

 

Among the many reports provided to help you analyze purchasing data you can find: 
 

 ·        Vendor reports: View which vendors supply which parts, compare vendor price lists and price quotes, and analyze the general performance of a given vendor (e.g., late receipts, rejected parts). 
 

 ·        Purchase Requisition reports: View open purchase requisitions, parts needed per vendor, and projected inventory balances that take into account open purchase requisitions and orders. 
 

 ·        Purchase Order reports: View open purchase orders, supply delays relating to a given vendor or part, price deviations in purchase orders, and all inventory transactions involving the items in a given purchase order. 
 

 ·        MRP and Purchase Planning reports: View analyses of MRP results, projected material balances and purchase recommendations based on anticipated material shortages. 
 
## Executive Reports

 

Use these summary reports to calculate and view purchase order and vendor invoice data for a given period. You can perform a variety of analyses by varying the data cross-sections that are retrieved, the manner in which they are sorted and their levels of hierarchy. BI reports allow you to compare purchase order and purchase invoice data from different years, periods and companies in various graphic formats. You can also determine the exact cross-section of data to be displayed in the data cube, by drilling down within categories and accessing only the specific data that you want to see. 
 

Furthermore, Priority provides easy-to-use report generators that allow you to create an unlimited number of custom reports utilizing both purchase order and vendor invoice data.
