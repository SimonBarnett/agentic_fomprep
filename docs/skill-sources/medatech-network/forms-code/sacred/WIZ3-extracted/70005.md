# Inventory Main Wizard

 
## Introduction

 

 Priority provides you with the tools you need to manage all aspects of inventory control. For example: 
 

1) Record the details of each item that you sell in your part catalogue. Create a list of part specifications appropriate to the goods in your catalogue, for easy data retrieval. 
 

2) Define the warehouses you need, such as holding warehouses, kit warehouses, project warehouses and others for branch offices and subcontractors. 
 

3) Create bills of materials for parts you are manufacturing. 
 

4) Receive incoming raw materials, perform laboratory tests on selected samples and produce certificates of analysis (COAs). Release approved materials into inventory as numbered lots with a defined shelf life. Return any rejected materials to the vendor. 
 

5) Receive processed parts from subcontractors. 
 

6) Assemble raw materials and/or processed parts into finished products in a warehouse, or process them on the factory floor. If necessary, transfer goods from one warehouse to another. 
 

7) Record any malfunctioning parts and refer them to the appropriate material review board. 
 

8) Open serial numbers for the parts and/or their component parts, and track the inventory transactions of each serialized part. 
 

9) Pack the goods into crates and store them in a holding warehouse. 
 

10) Ship your product to your customers. 
 

11) Receive any goods returned by the customers. 
 

12) Record a past balance for a chosen date and run a new inventory count. 
 
## Note: 

 

Use various BPM tools to track the progress of inventory documents, streamline the inventory process and increase the flow of information between employees handling these documents. 
 
## Using This Wizard

 

The following sections outline various aspects of inventory management. 
 

Click the hypertext links to open specialized wizards designed to assist you in the performance of specific inventory tasks. Each wizard features an introductory page providing a brief overview of the subject, followed by detailed instructions. Most of these wizards are also accessible from the main Priority Inventory menu and its sub-menus. 
 
## Parts

 

 Priority enables you to create your own part catalogue , in which you can record all the parts and services your company provides to its customers. You can define important parameters for each part in the catalogue, such as part type, buy/sell unit and factory unit and their conversion ratios, bar code, list price and purchase price. You can also assign a part to a part family, whose members share certain common properties and can be accessed collectively in numerous reports. 
 

You can facilitate data retrieval by defining a series of part specifications, which identify parts by their distinguishing characteristics (e.g., model, year of manufacture, color). The Part Specs Work Area allows you to derive new parts from combinations of part specifications, and assign them composite part numbers and descriptions based on specification values. 
 
## Warehouses

 

 Priority provides three predefined warehouses: Plant Floor, Main (Factory Warehouse) and Warehouse Outlet. You can add to these by defining new warehouses , and assign them as defaults for various inventory transactions. Default warehouses appear automatically in the relevant inventory transaction documents. 
 

You can define a warehouse to serve as a temporary holding warehouse for goods that have already been sold to a customer (i.e., for which a sales invoice has already been prepared). Goods stored in a holding warehouse are considered customer inventory and are not taken into account during inventory counts, valuation or costing. 
 
## Inventory Documents

 

Each inventory transaction must be recorded in a document (e.g., goods receiving voucher, laboratory release, warehouse assembly). You can set up inventory documents to suit your specific needs by defining a variety of default properties and assigning custom numeration templates to each document type. You can also define which users are allowed access to documents recorded for a given branch or sales rep, or for secured customers and vendors. 
 
## Incoming Inventory

 

 Priority provides two alternative methods of recording the receipt of goods from vendors and sub-contractors: 
 

 ·        A goods receiving voucher (GRV) increases the inventory levels of the goods received, and can be itemized automatically on the basis of purchase orders or even invoices (in the case of advance payment). 
 

 ·        A vendor invoice both increases inventory levels and charges you for the received goods. 
 

 ·        A special return document is provided to record the return of goods to a vendor (or to a subcontractor for reworking), though a GRV can also be used for this purpose, using negative quantities. 
 

If you work with lots, you can define lot parameters for a specific raw material, including its shelf life and the numeration template used to assign incoming shipments of the material automatically to lots. A material’s shelf life can be extended (e.g., on the basis of laboratory testing), subject to its predefined extension limit. 
 

 Note: In the dual-currency package, you can record the release of imported inventory from bonded warehouses and their receipt into your own warehouses. 
 
## Laboratory Analysis

 

You can require certain incoming parts and materials to undergo laboratory testing before they are accepted into inventory. Priority enables you to record the sampling of small quantities of these parts for testing purposes, and document the test results. You can attach a Certificate of Analysis (COA) to any inventory transaction or production control document, in order to establish the quality level of the parts in question. The COA includes the results of the tests those parts are required to undergo. 
 
## Manufactured and Assembled Parts

 

If your company manufactures or assembles a product, you need to define its bill of materials . The bill of materials lists the product’s component parts and their ratio to the finished product. 
 

A warehouse assembly document is used to report the assembly of a part from its component parts in the warehouse. Recording an assembly document automatically increases the inventory balance of the assembled part and decreases the balance of its component parts proportionally. 
 

 Note: 
 

Issues of parts and materials from warehouses to the factory floor, production reports and floor inventory counts are discussed in various production wizards. 
 

 Priority provides a mechanism for handling malfunctions that were detected during production or discovered in inventoried parts. Malfunctions that cannot be fixed by the appointed quality personnel are referred to a material review board (MRB) responsible for solving the problem. 
 

You can open serial numbers for manufactured or assembled parts and their components, either independently or while recording inventory transactions. Serial numbers can be attached to parts at any stage, from receipt of goods from vendors to customer shipment. You can also create a catalogue of serialized parts, in which you can view all data relating to a specific instance of a part, including any serialized component parts, its audit trail, details of the customer that purchased it, its warranty and service history. 
 
## Fashion Assortments

 

If you are working with the Fashion module, you will be creating assortments of parts that share the same style, but differ in color and size (and possibly other parameters). Inventory transactions for fashion assortments are itemized via a two-dimensional matrix, where the X-axis represents the possible size values and the Y-axis represents the possible color values. In each case, a basic assortment ratio is defined, and the final transaction quantity is based on the number of assortments involved. 
 
## Outgoing Inventory

 

 Priority allows you to record a packing slip for goods that have been packed and set aside, either for shipment to a customer or for stock. You can include several crates, each with its own packing slip, in a main packing crate, and attach it to a principal packing slip. 
 

As in the receipt of goods, customer shipments can be recorded in one of two ways: 
 

 ·        A customer shipment document decreases the inventory levels of the shipped goods, and can be itemized automatically on the basis of sales orders. A sales invoice both decreases inventory levels and charges the customer for the shipped goods. Either of these documents can be used as a bill of lading when you are shipping packed crates. 
 

 ·        A special return document is provided to record the return of goods from a customer, though a customer shipment document can also be used for this purpose, using negative quantities. 
 

You can also document the transfer of goods between warehouses (e.g., to fill a sales order in a company branch). 
 

A fictitious warehouse can be specified as a consignment/loan warehouse , so that you can record the shipment of goods on consignment or on loan. Though the goods are physically stored by the customer, they are recorded as inventory of this warehouse, where they continue to be included in inventory valuations and costing reports. 
 
## Inventory Counts

 

Before you begin working with Priority , you must record initial inventory levels , based on a physical inventory count. These recorded inventory levels are the point of departure for all future inventory transactions. 
 

Regular inventory counts calculate warehouse balances for a particular date, based on the past balance and the inventory transactions recorded in the system. These balances can then be compared to physical counts and the variance displayed. 
 

You can record a past balance that “locks” the inventory balances in all warehouses on a given date in the past. This prevents any retroactive revision of warehouse balances, and provides a point of reference for inventory counts and reports, as well as costing calculations. 
 
## Inventory Reports

 

Among the many reports provided to help you analyze inventory data you can find: 
 

 ·        Part reports: View data relating to parts and their parameters, part families, preferred vendors, part availability and more. 
 

 ·        Inventory reports: View inventory levels per warehouse, part or family, pending inventory transaction documents and more. 
 

 ·        Inventory Transaction reports: View detailed transaction reports (i.e., per transaction type) or transaction summaries (e.g., inventory journal, summary of transaction quantities, summary of customer shipments). 
 

 ·        Additional reports cover data relating to engineering, malfunctions (MRB), serialized parts and bills of materials. 
 

 ·        You can also generate customized reports relating either to the part catalogue or to serialized parts.
