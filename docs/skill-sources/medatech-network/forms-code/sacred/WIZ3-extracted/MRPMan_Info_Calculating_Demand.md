# Calculating Demand for Final Products

 

 Priority offers two basic methods for calculating demand; the method applied to each part is determined by its Replenishment Type ( R or B ): 
 

 ·        Replenishment Type R: The quantity of parts required for part sales is calculated according to actual sales orders and forecast orders. This is called dynamic demand . If the part is manufactured, you can set up the program to calculate demand for a part according to its work orders, rather than to sales orders. 
 

 ·        Replenishment Type B: Parts of this type are required in fixed quantities over regular periods. This is called static demand . Purchased parts of this type do not generate issues in MRP; rather, purchase planning handles them directly according to the part's planning definitions (e.g., time frame, quantities). 
 

In addition to the demand for shippable goods, MRP also takes into account the quantities of safety stock defined for both types of parts. 
 

The replenishment type affects the calculation of direct demand (generated by work orders/sales orders or from routine consumption of the part itself ). In both cases, demand for the part is added to the indirect demand generated by planned or open work orders for a parent part. 
 
## Demand for child parts

 

MRP expands the BOM of each processed part and calculates the required quantity of child parts according to the defined parent/child ratios. If the child part is a sub-assembly, the same process is carried out for its child parts, and any further child parts. Work orders are then planned for all processed child parts on all levels of the BOM.
