# Defining a Bill of Materials

 

A bill of materials (BOM) lists the raw materials or sub-assemblies required to assemble or manufacture a processed (type P ) part, and their required quantities. The processed part is called the parent part, and its components are called child parts. 
 

Before you define a bill of materials, make sure that all parts (parent and children) are recorded in the Part Catalogue form. Parent parts are usually type P , while child parts are either type R (raw material) or type P (sub-assembly). You may also define a child part for a type R part, when you want to record an accompanying part (i.e., an accessory or spec that ships with the purchased part). 
 
## To define a bill of materials:

 

 1 .  Enter the Part Catalogue form and retrieve the desired parent part.
 Note: At any point in the following process, you can transfer handling of the part to another employee, by specifying his/her username in the Assigned to column (in the Additional Details tab). 
 

 2 .  Enter the Child Parts sub-level form and itemize the child parts. 
 

 3 .  For every child part, indicate the quantity of children required for one unit of the parent in the Qty(Child) column.
 Notes: 
 

 ·      If the child part is a sub-assembly, you must also treat it as a parent and define its child parts in the same manner. 
 

 ·      If you are recording an accompanying part (i.e., the parent part is type R ), the Info Only column is flagged automatically. 
 

 4 .  Click to place restrictions on customizations of the BOM. 
 
## Notes:

 

 ·        To view the BOM of one or more specific parts, run the Part Explorer . 
 

 ·        To view BOM data in a variety of cross-sections, run the reports in the Bill of Materials Reports menu, a sub-level of the Part Catalogue menu.
