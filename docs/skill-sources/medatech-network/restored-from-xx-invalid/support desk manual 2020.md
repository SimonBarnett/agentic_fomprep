# support desk manual 2020

- **Source:** `support desk manual 2020.docx`

Introduction

Our role on Support is to ensure that issues raised by customers are captured and resolved promptly. Users of Priority can raise queries with support through phone calls, email or through our web portal.

We record the query in our Service Call form. Service Calls are prioritised based in severity and date opened.

This document aims to cover the procedures we undertake on support to ensure we meet the service level agreements between Medatech UK and the customer.

Opening a Service Call

Note: all service or assistance provided to the customer should be recorded in the system, including the issue, the provided solution and the time spent by each technician.

Manual Form Entry

When a customer raises a service call over the phone, the technician should record a service call in our system.

Open Service > Service Calls > Service Call and select the relevant Customer, Contact, Severity, Contract, Project & Type of Call.

Type in a meaningful description (this will be the reference for the customer).

The Assigned to will populate with your username. You can change this to a different technician to investigate the call, or to a screener if it still needs screening.

The Malfunction will populate with “Not Identified” and should be changed when the malfunction is known.

The Call Source will populate with Email but should be changed in this instance to Telephone.

If you are opening a service call on behalf of someone at Medatech, you can assign them as the “raised by” so that they will be informed of any progress.

Now fill in the external dialogue with details of the issue raised. You should then change the call status from New to Helpdesk. An email will now send to the contact with the call number, details and external call dialogue.

Outlook Sync

Customers can email their issues to support@medatechuk.com. We synchronise their emails into Priority, which will raise a service call for the contact.

In Outlook, right-click on the email you wish to synchronise. Click on the option to Synchronise with Priority. In the dialogue box which appears, choose the customer and select synchronise with Priority and open service call. You can also select a technician here otherwise the user who synchronises the emails will be assigned the call.

To synchronise any emails you have prepared for sync, in Priority navigate to Mail > Mail Options > Outlook > Synchronise with Outlook and then select “sync now”.

To retrieve calls opened through sync, query for any calls which are in New status.

Fill out the remaining details and set the call to Helpdesk. To meet our SLAs, you should change the call to Helpdesk within one hour of the email reaching the support inbox.

Note: Priority will open a new call providing there does not include an existing service call number in the subject of the email. Otherwise the email will synchronise with this existing call.

Web Service call

Customers can login to our service call portal, https://support.medatechuk.com/servicenew/, to raise and update service calls. These are automatically raised in our system with call source Web. We receive a notification by email that there is a new web call.

We can find this by querying for calls in New status. We should then fill out the mandatory details and assign it to a technician. The call can then be set to Helpdesk.

Dealing with active service calls

Working on a service call

To see active calls that are assigned to you, open Service > Service Calls > Active Service Calls and query for your user in “assigned to”.

To see service calls which are on your to-do-list, open Office > Calendar Maint. & Office Hours > To Do List, and query for Service Calls in “document type” and “assigned to” you. You should be working on these calls.

To begin working on a service call, open the call in the Service Calls form and select “take service call”. This will change the “in progress” field to your username and assigns you a blank line in the labour form. This will help you accurately log your time and will ensure that no-one works on the same call as you. Once you have finished working on the call, populate the end time in the call labour form. This will calculate your time spent.

Customers will not see anything written in the internal dialogue so use this space to write any notes and to communicate internally. Anything written in the external dialogue will be sent to the customer.

When you change the status of the service call, the “in progress” is removed.

Internal Escalation

When you reach a point of escalating the service call to a developer or project manager, write your communication to the project manager in the internal dialogue, add them as the “escalated to” and change the status to “esc to PM”. Add a task in their calendar for when you want them to look at the call.

To save time, do any investigation you can to help the project manager and detail this in the internal dialogue.

Communication should be done through support. Before escalating the call, make sure you notify the customer that their call is being looked at by a developer. When the developer has finished with the call, they should assign it back to helpdesk with details in the internal dialogue. Support will then communicate with the customer.

External Escalation

After investigation, you might decide that a call should be escalated to Priority Software. You should document the issue including details about the customer’s current version and get this approved internally before raising a call on the Priority software website.

You will be assigned a service call number, which you should paste in the “Priority service call” field on the internal service call. Paste any communication with Priority in the internal dialogue and let the customer know you have escalated the call.

Once Priority respond, change the call to Helpdesk so that it is back on your to-do list.

Closing a Service Call

Once both the customer and you are happy that the service call has been answered and that the resolution is detailed in the call, change the status of the call to “answered”. This will email the customer that the call has been answered with details attached.

If you feel like it would be useful, you can make an FAQ directly form the service call form. This is a great way to sum up the issue and resolution in a concise format.

When changing the call to answered, change the malfunction and resolution code to best reflect the service call.

Closing Closed Review Calls

Once a service call has been in Answered for 1 day, the call will change to Closed Review. This gives the customer a chance to dispute the call before it closes.

Support should then review any closed review calls and change them to “closed”. Once closed, the call details cannot be changed, and the customer will not be able to update this service call. It is therefore important to ensure all details are correct before closing a call.

Things to check when closing closed reviews:

“Details” should reflect the problem in the service call. Consider someone searching for the service call in the future.

“Malfunction” and “resolution” should be accurate. The more specific you can be about the problem, the better. Use “no response from cust” when there is no resolution because the customer failed to respond.

“Type of call” should be specified if possible.

Verify “Call source” is correct to the best of your knowledge.

In “External Dialogue” check that we actioned anything we promised to do. Look for any images with text and type out the message in the “internal dialogue”. This is particularly useful when searching for error messages.

In “External Dialogue”, remove any duplicate/unnecessary email text.

Ensure the resolution is detailed in the external or internal call dialogue.

Check there is time recorded in “service report - labour”, which accurately represents the time spent on the service call. Follow-up on any time ticked as billable.

In “Task Log” check tasks are ticked as completed.

Description of each status and severity code

Call Status

Each status describes the current action on a service call. Some statuses will trigger an email to the customer when assigned. Below is the list of call statuses along with their use and any emails they trigger.

New

This is the status given to a service call when it is first raised. Calls should be promptly changed to helpdesk

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

Emails send internally when the call remains in New for over 1hr

An email is sent to support when a service call is raised through the service call portal

Helpdesk

This status means the call is with support. Calls in this status are included in our to-do list

Customer emails:

An email is sent to the customer stating that their call is with support

Internal emails:

An email is sent to the “raised by” to inform them that we opened a service call

An email is sent to the “assigned to” after 5 days of inactivity to ensure the customer is kept up to date

Client Action

Calls should be put in Client Action when there is currently no action with support, and we need something from the customer.

Customer emails:

An email is sent to the customer to say the call is awaiting their action

Internal emails:

A duplicate of the email sent to the customer is also sent to support

An email is sent to the “assigned to” after 5 days of inactivity to chase the customer for an update

Cust. Update

Any time the customer updates their service call, the status changes to Customer Update. This adds the call back to our to-do list if it was not there already.

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Web update

Like Cust. Update, a service call is assigned the status Web Update when the customer updates their service call through the web. The status automatically changes to Cust. Update

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Update

This is the status we use when we want to send an additional email to the customer. The status changes back to its previous status.

Customer emails:

An email is sent to the customer with the message “your service call has been updated”

Internal emails:

No internal emails are sent

Quote Required

When a service call is awaiting allocation of a quote, we set the status to Quote Required and assign the call to the user responsible for creating the quote. We then send the quote separately and change the call to Answered because any further work should be completed as a sales order.

Customer emails:

An email is sent to the customer letting them know their service call requires a quote

Internal emails:

An email is sent to the assigned to asking them to raise a quote

Esc to PM

We use this status when a service call is escalated internally

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

An email is sent to “escalated to”

Esc to Prio

We use this status when we raise a service call with Priority software

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Scheduled

We use this status to signify that we have scheduled this service call for the future. There should be a corresponding task made in the user’s calendar.

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Monitoring

Change the service call to monitoring if no further action is required from either the customer or support but the call is being monitored.

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Answered

Once a service call is complete and the resolution is logged, change the status to answered. No further action is required, and the call automatically closes after 1 day.

Customer emails:

The customer is emailed that their call has been answered.

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Closed Review

This is the initial closing status of a service call. Only calls which have been answered may enter closed review status. Support will periodically review these calls to check they contain all relevant details before closing them.

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

An email is sent to “raised by” to let them know the call has been closed

Closed

This is the final status of a service call. You cannot make any changes to a closed service call so you should make sure all the details and labour recorded is correct before closing the call

Customer emails:

No emails are sent to the customer

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Cancelled

If you open a service call in error, you may change the status to cancelled which will cancel the call.

Customer emails:

An email is sent to the customer to let them know their service call was cancelled. The email contains the call dialogue

Internal emails:

No emails are sent internally

Note: Service calls cannot be deleted, they should be cancelled instead.

Severity Codes

We assign each service call a severity code. Most calls will be assigned “Normal” severity, however alternative severity codes should be used when appropriate. This is important, as we should give higher priority to more severe service calls.

Critical

An issue that affects functionality or performance of the software, which prevents the customer from performing their normal business

Major

An issue that affects functionality or performance of the software, which makes it difficult for a customer to perform their normal business

Normal

An issue that affects functionality or performance of the software, which does not prevent the customer performing their normal business

Minor

An issue, request or minor defect, which does not affect functionality or performance of the software

Enquiry

A query not relating to a problem in the system
