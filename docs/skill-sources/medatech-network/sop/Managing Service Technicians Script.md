Introduction (00:00-01:25) 
This video addresses Priority's Service Management software, an all-in-one solution 
for companies that sell integrated systems -- such as security systems, HVAC 
(Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning) systems, elevator systems and the like -- 
and provide ongoing service for these systems. This solution enables you to manage 
all pre-sale and post-sale activities relating to the integrated systems you provide -- 
including warranty and maintenance of these systems -- all within a single software 
package. 
Priority's Service Management software helps companies oversee customer 
relations across the sales process; organize the various costs associated with the 
sales process within a single sales project; work with service contracts, and handle 
service calls: whether by help desk staff, or by field technicians. Moreover, a variety 
of financial tools are integrated into Priority's customer service solution, and various 
executive reports are available to help you manage the entire process. An overview 
of the entire solution is presented in a separate video clip. 
In this video, we will demonstrate one aspect of the solution: managing service 
technicians in Priority. 
At this point, we'll demonstrate how routine servicing is handled in Priority, including 
the logistics of managing both service technicians who work from the office and field 
service technicians. 
Opening a Service Call (01:26-03:47) 
Let's begin with the service call. From the Customer Service > Service Calls menu, 
enter the Service Calls form. As we will demonstrate shortly, all aspects of the 
service call can be handled directly from this form, including the reporting of parts, 
labor and other related expenses, as well as customer billing. 
There are several ways to record a new service call. 
One way is for the designated customer contact to log in to our web site and record 
their service call directly into the system as we have demonstrated with our web site. 
Alternatively, customers can e-mail a service representative when they experience a 
technical issue, and the e-mail message can be imported into Priority, from either 
Outlook or Gmail, as a service call. Upon synchronization, a service call is opened in 
the corresponding Priority form. The message's subject is copied into the Details 
column, its contents appear as the details of the malfunction and, if any files were 
attached to the message, such as photographs of the faulty part, these will be 
imported into the Attachments sub-level form. The relevant Customer No. is 
identified based on the e-mail address from which the message was sent. If the 
message's subject indicates the number of an existing service call to be updated, the

Providing Services Under a Service Contract (03:48-05:38) 
Before continuing to the next tab, we'll also indicate the Serial Number of the device 
to be serviced. If the specified device is under contract, the number of the 
corresponding service contract is filled in automatically. When services are provided 
under contract, this mechanism enables you to verify that the serial number on each 
device being serviced matches one of the serial numbers recorded in the service 
contract. This helps ensure that company technicians provide service only for the 
actual parts that you provided, or -- if you sell a servicing package for an existing 
system -- that the part in question has passed inspection by an authorized technician.  
Press F6 from this column to view all information pertaining to the serialized part in 
the Catalogue of Parts w/Serial Nos., including the date of installation at the 
customer site and the service terms that apply to the device. The Serialized 
Components of Part sub-level form lists any components that make up the device, 
some of which may also be assigned a serial number.let’s return to the open service 
call.  
Moving to the Service Info tab, note that the Service Terms defined for this device 
in the Catalogue of Parts w/Serial Nos. have now been filled in automatically. Note 
that you can press F6 from this column to access the Service Terms form, from 
which you can review the Conditions of Service Terms. . 
When recording a service call for a part that isn't assigned a serial number, record a 
service contract number manually. The service terms are generally filled in based on 
the corresponding service contract. If the part in question is not under contract, the 
default value, "Full Payment", is applied. This mechanism ensures that the customer 
is charged for billable items only, when an invoice is generated for the service call. 
Technician Scheduling (05:39-08:45) 
In the Scheduling tab, we'll note that the call is also automatically Assigned to  a 
default technician, based on the defined part family, zone or customer - depending 
on how default settings are defined. In any case, we can reassign the call as needed, 
and even assign a second and third technician to a given call if necessary. 
Scheduled service calls are also recorded automatically as tasks assigned to the 
relevant technicians.  
You may also want to keep track of the labor devoted to a given call at a finer 
resolution, by dividing the handling time into multiple tasks. To do so, use the Task

Note that you can also define color-coding for service calls based on the values of 
designated columns. For example, you may decide to display any urgent service 
calls in the color red. 
From the list of Direct Activations provided in this form, we'll select the Technician 
Scheduling Calendar to check which of the company's technicians are available to 
handle this call over the next few days. When the calendar is activated directly from 
a specific call, any assignment recorded here] will be linked automatically to that call. 
Press F1 from any scheduled item to view additional details, such as customer and 
part information.  
You can also access the Technician Scheduling Calendar from the Service Calls 
menu. Conversely, you can view or update the details of a scheduled call in the 
Service Calls form by pressing F2 from the relevant calendar item. Let's exit the 
calendar and return to the Service Calls form. Note that the call's Start Time and 
End Time have been filled in automatically with the times we defined in the calendar. 
The entire scheduling process can be performed from either the Technician 
Scheduling Calendar or the Service Calls form; the calendar provides a graphic 
visualization of the schedules of multiple technicians. Note that Priority calendars 
can also be synchronized with your Outlook or Gmail calendar. 
Another tool that can help you manage technician scheduling is the Service 
Manager Dashboard, accessed from the Executive Reports > Customer Service 
menu. In the Service Calls Requiring Attn web part, we'll choose to view service 
calls by technician. The Pie display illustrates any imbalance in the current allocation 
of technicians to active service calls, enabling you to find and eliminate any 
bottlenecks. 
Yet another report that can be useful in this regard is the Service Call Load report, 
which enables you to view the daily, weekly or monthly service call load for a 
designated period. 
BPM for Service Calls (08:46-09:34) 
The Service Calls form also provides access to a business process management or 
BPM flow chart for service calls, which enables you to define rules that determine 
how and when call statuses are updated, and set up notifications such as automated 
e-mails that are sent to a designated user when service calls receive a given status. 
For example, in our company we'
ve defined a rule for the "Completed" status 
ensuring that SMS notification is sent to the relevant customer contact when a 
service call receives the "Completed" status. 
Let's proceed to review Priority's field service tools, which can be accessed from a 
variety of handheld devices, mobile phones and laptop computers.

Viewing Scheduled Service Calls (10:00-11:38) 
From the main screen, the field technician can open a lis
t of active service calls that 
are currently assigned to him or her. This list contains basic information about these 
active calls, including the scheduled date and time for each. The context-sensitive 
menu for this screen presents options for filtering the displayed service calls, or 
viewing a map overlay of scheduled service calls by customer name. 
Let's select the next scheduled service call and click the Details icon. In addition to 
details pertaining to the reported malfunction, this screen also displays the 
customer's address and phone number. The phone icon enables technicians to place 
a call to this number using their handheld device, while clicking the map icon opens 
a map of the area in Google Maps.  
Next, we'll examine the customer's description of the malfunction. Clicking the 
exclamation mark "!" icon opens a window displaying additional malfunction details. 
This screen also displays the terms of service currently in effect for the serialized 
part being serviced, which ultimately determine whether the customer will be billed 
for these services. 
Let's go back to the previous screen. From here, technicians can access a history of 
malfunctions reported for the same serialized part, and see what actions were taken 
at that time, as well as a list of parts that were used or returned during servicing. 
Managing Inventory in the Field (11:39-13:02) 
If, after reading up on the faulty part and its service history, the technician concludes 
that the device itself or one of its component
s needs to be replaced, he or she can 
proceed from the call itself to the Parts form. Here they can choose the necessary 
part from a list of all inventory currently stored in their service vehicle. If this is a 
serialized part, pressing the down arrow opens a list of specific devices, sorted by 
serial number.  
If the technician's vehicle lacks sufficient inventory of any required parts, they can 
check if it's possible to transfer inventory from a transit warehouse before travelling 
to the customer. Note that technicians can also use their mobile device to open a 
purchase demand for any parts that are running low in stock. 
Let's return to our service call. Click Contacts to view the customer contact 
associated with this call and click the phone icon to dial his number and let him know 
you're on your way over. Note the car icon indicating that the call is "In Progress", 
that is, the technician is en route to the customer or on site.

Under the heading Status Log, you can see the status changes recorded for the

change the status to "Completed" so that the parts, actions and times recorded for 
the call are registered in the system, while adding the customer's signature as

Once the technician returns to the office, he can use the Priority Rich interface to 
perform additional actions, such as researching similar cases using Priority's 
Enterprise Search tool, which offers full text search in any and all system 
documents and attachments, or automatically preparing an invoice for the service 
call by running the relevant program from the list of Direct Activations in that form. 
Note that the invoices generated for service calls automatically take into account any 
relevant service terms and valid warranties on serviced parts.
