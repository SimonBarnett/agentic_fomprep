Introduction (00:00-01:30) 
In this video, we will demonstrate Priority's Service Management software, an all-in-
one solution for companies that sell integrated systems – such as security systems, 
HVAC (Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning) systems, elevator systems and the 
like – and provide ongoing service for these systems. This solution enables you to 
manage all pre-sale and post-sale activity relating to the integrated systems you 
provide – including warranty and maintenance of these systems – all within a single 
software package.  
We'll begin with a brief demonstration of how to use Priority to oversee customer 
relations across the sales process. We'll then see how to organize the various costs 
associated with the sales process within a single sales project; work with service 
contracts, and handle service calls: whether handled by help desk staff, or by field 
technicians. Finally, we'll briefly review the financial tools integrated into Priority's 
customer service tools, as well as various executive reports that can help you 
manage the entire process. 
Note that Priority can be installed and run on both desktop and notebook computers, 
either locally or over the Internet. The system can also be accessed from a variety of 
mobile devices running a variety of operating systems, ranging from rugged 
handheld devices to mobile phones and tablet computers. 
CRM-Customer Relations Management (01:31-03:13)  
Priority supports the entire sales
 process, from recording leads, through opening 
sales opportunities, to issuing quotes and processing orders.  
Sales and marketing personnel who work with the customer client can open the 
Customer Dashboard to see an overview of annual sales to the customer, the 
customer's financial obligations, customer quotes and opportunities and, of particular 
interest, service calls. This last item allows the employee to ensure that there are no 
active service calls for the customer before making a phone call offering a new 
product. If there are open service calls, the salesperson knows to call and inquire 
about the status of the call, and to make sure the customer is satisfied with the 
service being provided, before offering a new product.  
The Sales Rep Dashboard displays estimated sales forecasts, open sales 
opportunities, actual sales orders and customer invoices, among other options. You 
can display the data that are pertinent to the individual sales rep only or include data 
for the entire sales team. 
All CRM functions are also available when accessing Priority on your mobile device. 
Thus, for example, a sales rep who receives an e-mail on his smartphone or tablet 
requesting an update on a particular sales opportunity can click the link in the e-mail

Sales Projects (03:14-05:36) 
All sales related activities can be linked to a single project, beginning with the initial 
price quote, enabling y
ou to keep track of all relevant expenses from within the sales 
project. Employees can report their progress on various related activities, such as 
taking measurements or system installation, directly from the project record. If the 
setup or installation requires the services of an external subcontractor, such as an 
electrical engineer, the corresponding purchase orders can be linked to the project 
as well. By recording all actual and projected expenses against the project in this 
manner, you can begin to cost and scope the project even at the pre-sales stage.  
In the Reports of Project Hrs/Expenses sub-level form, we can see all the work 
hours that have been reported at any stage of the process; in this case, reports have 
been entered by both the sales rep who works with this customer and the technician 
who took measurements at the customer sites.  
The Revenue and Profit form displays a summary of all costs and revenue 
associated with the sales project. Moving to the Projected Rev tab, note that the 
revenue currently stands at zero, as no sales order has been received yet and no 
goods have been shipped to the customer. Under the Projected Cost tab, you can 
see any costs associated with, say, purchase orders that were opened for the project 
but have not yet been billed. Under Actual Cost, you see how much the project has 
actually cost so far; in this case, due to the labor reported by employees who have 
worked on the project. Later on, the costs listed here will also include any labor and 
parts that are expended on service calls recorded during the initial warranty period, 
which is included in the original sale. Thus, at the end of the initial warranty period, 
we'll be able to see whether the transaction as a whole has been profitable. 
Once you succeed in closing the sale, you can open a service contract for the project 
directly from the Projects form.Let's proceed to review the service contract we've 
prepared for this project and see what options are available. 
Service Contracts (05:37-06:28) 
The service contract determines the period in whic
h services are provided for each 
individual part or assembled component in the system under the initial warranty. At 
the end of this period, the customer will be charged for annual maintenance.  
In the References tab, we're going to update the Service Terms for this contract to 
"Service for New Systems". Consequently, work hours will be covered by the overall 
contract fee, but parts will not be. 
In the Items in Contract sub-level form, you can see a list of components included 
in this contract and the charges for each. To ensure that our technicians provide 
services only for the actual parts that we provided, we've used the Serial Number 
column to specify individual serialized parts, such as electrical components.

Moving to the Service Info tab, note that the Service Terms defined for this device 
have been filled in automatically. This mechanism ensures that the customer is

In the Scheduling tab, we'll note that the call is also automatically Assigned to  a 
default technician, based on default settings that you define ahead of time. We can 
also assign a second and third technician to a given call if necessary.  
In some cases, you may also want to divide the labor devoted to a given call into 
multiple tasks, to be handled by one or more different technicians, or other staff 
members. Such tasks can be opened directly from the Task List sub-level form. 
Note that you can also define color-coding for service calls, based on the values of 
designated columns. For example, you may decide to display any urgent service 
calls in the color red. 
From the list of Direct Activations provided in this form, we'll select the Technician 
Scheduling Calendar to check when the company's technicians are available to 
handle this call over the next few days. Press F1 from any scheduled item to view 
additional details, such as customer and part information.  
Let's exit the calendar and return to the Service Calls form. Note that the call's Start 
Time and End Time have been filled in automatically with the times we defined in 
the calendar. Note that Priority calendars can also be synchronized with your 
Outlook or Gmail calendar. 
Another tool that can help you manage technician scheduling is the Service 
Manager Dashboard. In the Service Calls Requiring Attn web part, we'll choose to 
view service calls by technician. The Pie display illustrates any imbalance in the 
current allocation of technicians to active service calls, enabling you to find and 
eliminate any bottlenecks. Note the other view options available in the choose list on 
the bottom-left corner, for instance, service calls by type of malfunction.  
Yet another report that can be useful in this regard is the Service Call Load report, 
which enables you to view the daily, weekly or monthly service call load for a 
designated period. 
The Service Calls form also provides access to a business process management or 
BPM flow chart for service calls, which enables you to define rules that determine 
how and when call statuses are updated, and set up notifications such as automated 
e-mails that are sent to a designated user when service calls receive a given status. 
For example, in our company we've defined a rule for the "Completed" status 
ensuring that SMS notification is sent to the relevant customer contact when a 
service call receives the "Completed" status.

From the main screen, the field technician can open a list of active service calls that 
are currently assigned to him or her. This list contains basic information about these 
active calls, including the scheduled date and time for each. The context-sensitive 
menu for this screen presents options for filtering the displayed service calls, or 
viewing a map overlay of scheduled calls. 
Once the technician arrives at the customer site, he or she can use the available 
forms to view additional details pertaining to the reported malfunction and the 
serialized part being serviced,  to report any actions taken to repair the malfunction, 
and to report any parts that are used or returned during servicing. Finally, the 
technician can report the time spent on this service call, including travel time.

Executive Reports (12:14-14:02) 
Finally, let's review a few executive reports and BI tools that are available to the 
service manager. 
One such tool, which we saw earlier in this video, is the Service Manager 
Dashbo
ard. From this Dashboard, we can view data for open service calls in a 
variety of cross-sections. We can also use this Dashboard to check the Technician 
Scheduling Calendar and to get an overview of the service provided to a particular 
customer. 
Next, we'll use the Executive Reports menu to run a BI report that can be used for 
service call analysis. Here too, data can be presented in various cross-sections, 
such as customer group. You can then continue to drill down within the data to 
obtain more specific cross-sections of data. Click on any of the provided links to view 
more detailed information, or access individual records in Priority. 
You can also generate your own customized reports, using the provided report 
generators.  
Finally, there are a number of service contract reports that can be used to analyze 
the profitability of service contracts. These reports present your actual profit from 
designated service contracts, after deducting expenses such as free services and 
replacement parts that were provided under contract. 
This concludes our summary of Priority's all-in-one solution for integrated systems 
suppliers, which enables companies to handle the pre-sale process, installation 
project, service contract and on-going customer service, and billing within a single 
software package, with no add-ons.
