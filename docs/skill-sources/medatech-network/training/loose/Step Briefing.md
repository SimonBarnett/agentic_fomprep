Getting the most from your Step Internship

Part one of this briefing contains important information about your Step internship.  Make sure you read it BEFORE you start your internship and if you have any questions contact your Step adviser.

Part two provides you with a template and guidance for keeping a Step Journal.

Part One:  The Essentials

Welcome to the Step Programme!

Your internship will begin shortly and you should have been contacted by your Step adviser with details of working hours, location and dress code. If you are unsure about any of these aspects then please contact your Step adviser immediately.

You should receive an induction at the start of the internship period. The induction should provide you with a good understanding of your duties and the outcomes expected. You should also understand the management structure and have a named supervisor or line manager.

Change of details

It is important you notify Step immediately if you change your address or telephone number so we can keep in contact with you.  You should also contact Step as a priority if you change your bank account details in order to avoid any delays or problems in receiving your pay.

Reporting sickness or absence

Under the Terms of your Step Internship Contract or Agreement you must notify both your Supervisor and Step as soon as possible of your absence due to sickness, and no later than 10am on the morning of your sickness.

To notify Step, call your Step adviser and  leave details of:

Your name

Your company

The reason for your absence

When you expect to return to continue your internship at the company.

You will not receive any pay for the days you are absent.  You can, however choose to take these as holiday, in agreement with your supervisor and Step.

It is essential to keep your supervisor and Step updated on the circumstances of your absence.

On the first day you return to your host company after a period of sickness absence you must complete a Sickness Absence Form (on page 6 of this briefing) , get it countersigned by your line manager  and then send it to your Step adviser. You can scan and email the form.

Authorised Absence/Holiday Request

Use the Authorised Absence/Holiday Request Form (on page 5 of this briefing) to record all absences, including Bank Holidays.   Ensure your supervisor approves every absence.

You must return the Authorised Absence/Holiday Request Form to your Step adviser at least 5 working days before the end of your internship.

Changes to your internship

If the company informs you of any change in the specification of the internship or tells you that the assignment will be shorter than originally planned, please let your Step adviser know straight away who will confirm any changes.

Health and Safety Advice

It is important you are aware of the Health and Safety at Work Act.  This is a law that protects people at work and the general public.

We all have a legal as well as a moral obligation to comply with the health and safety issues that may affect us and also the people around us.

Step is committed to the Health and Safety at Work Act and the aim of this briefing is to make you aware of your responsibilities as well as our responsibilities.

Our responsibilities…

We will always try to match your capabilities and skills to your assignments and the needs of our clients.

As far as possible and as is reasonably practical, Step will do all they can to ensure you have a safe workplace and receive the relevant health and safety instruction and training.

The company you are assigned to is responsible for your health and safety while you are working with them.  Step will ensure you are given information on safety procedures so that you can carry out your tasks safely.

If you are unsure or do not understand anything please ask for clarification.

Your responsibilities…

On the first day of your assignment ask about the company safety policy.

Observe and co-operate with the safety procedures of the company and ensure you never attempt to operate unfamiliar equipment or undertake tasks you believe are unsafe.

Know the procedures to adopt in the event of a fire and familiarise yourself with the fire exits.

Report any potential hazardous situations to your on-site contact.

Ensure you report all accidents or incidents to your on-site contact (however trivial they may seem).

Please ensure you report any accident to Step.

Take care of your health and safety as well as that of others who may be affected by your work.

Maximising Potential

Your Step internship is a great opportunity to immerse yourself in the culture of your host company. Step  have taken every step to ensure this internship is development focussed but alongside this there may be the requirement to complete more mundane tasks or to work late to meet deadlines. If you are invited to help in this way, ensure you maximise the potential of each situation. This will provide an insight into the real world of working, from business breakfast meetings, to Friday afternoon company socials. Whatever the situation, there is always something to be learnt.

Complaints / Queries

Occasionally things can go wrong and it is imperative that you contact us as soon as you have any concerns about the internship. It is our job to ensure a satisfactory outcome for both parties. If this situation arises we ask you to contact us first to talk through the best way of handling the situation.

We are here to help and have experience in resolving the kind of issues that can arise during an internship of this type. Please give us the chance to try and resolve the situation and if the internship has to be ended early, of handling the matter in a professional way.

Assessment and evaluation

At the end of your internship you are invited to complete an assessment and evaluation form which should be completed with your supervisor; a copy of the evaluation form should be sent to Step.  This is in addition to your Step Journal.

This will be a useful reference for you when applying for future roles.

In addition to the evaluation form, we welcome more informal blog contributions from our interns to describe their Step experience.  These will be published on our site.  If you are a twitter user, we would also like you to tweet abou your first day and/or send us a picture – clearly you must get you company’s approval before you do this!  We will also invite you to join the Step Alumni Group on LinkedIn this is a good place to share issues that you might encounter during your internship, to seek comment and views from other Step interns.

We have also linked up with Inside Buzz – part of Target Jobs to compile an ongoing review of the Step programme.  We will contact you towards the end of your placement to invite you to complete an online survey.

And finally …

Please act and dress in a professional and business-like manner at all times in-keeping with your working environment.

Part Two:  Your Step Journal

Your Step internship is both a training opportunity and a chance to show the value of your work. Broadly speaking you can expect to be working to the same daily schedule as permanent employees. We encourage you to allow sometime each week for critical reflection, say over a lunch break or at the end of your working day.

Journal

We recommend that you keep a Step internship journal to record and assess your professional and personal development. The entries in here should prove invaluable when updating your Step Profile or CV. The process of writing will also help you reflect on what you have learned.

A template for a Step internship Journal  is attached to this document, and here are a few tips:

Set aside a regular time for entries, even if it’s only a few minutes each week;

The more time you spend on this the more value you will get back in the future;

Keep it safe;

The format doesn’t matter: online, hand-held device; notepad, computer file. Choose the most convenient.

Your Step internship journal is meant to be a helpful tool in learning and developing, and it may be helpful to share with others, but you can also choose to keep it private.

Topics/questions to consider

At the outset

Give an honest assessment of your skills and experience highlighting those areas which you consider to be your strongest and weakest.

What are your expectations from this experience?

What are you most worried about?

What are you most looking forward to?

During your Step internship

What do you like most about your work?

What do you like least?

Do you understand the business and aims of the organisation and are you aware of how you are contributing?

How would you describe your relationship with your supervisor or line manager?

How would you describe your relationship with your fellow workers?

What is the company culture like and do you fit in well?

What is your typical working day like?

What projects have you completed that you are most proud of?

How have your experiences compared to your expectations?

After your Step internship

How did your academic background and qualifications help your work?

How did any previous work experience help?

What were the high points of the internships?

What were the low points?

Did the internship meet your expectations?

How has the internship made an impact on your professional and personal development?

Would you change anything if you could start the internship again?
